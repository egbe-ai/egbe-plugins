# Execute and verify the implementation

Build the smallest real vertical path first, then supporting work. Use actual
domain contracts and follow the plan's scope; code, types and tests around an
absent central result do not complete it. Inspect existing completion claims as
leads, not proof. A time limit leaves work resumable, not permission to narrow
the promise or substitute a demo.

## Direct verification

Match evidence to the claim. Source/local/contract tests can prove their boundary;
a serving journey needs evidence from the serving revision. Build, lint, HTTP 200,
data shape and a successful checkout redirect are supporting checks, not proof
of the customer's result. Inspect generated/transformed content for the accepted
quality on meaningful inputs, not just schema validity.

- Exercise relevant intermediate, partial, failure and recovery states as well
  as success. Check authoritative state and rendered guidance/actions together;
  a later successful action does not prove the earlier guidance was correct.
- For coupled delivery/state changes, test the actual joining boundary under
  duplicates, retries, concurrency and relevant partial failures. An isolated
  reducer or sequential simulation cannot establish the whole operation.
- Verify the accepted access policy on the real result, including direct API,
  file and link access; prove guest-to-account continuity without cross-account
  takeover. Use Auth's normal authority and preserve its separate account-entry proof.
- When updating stored customer work, retain and recover an original record/file
  across the update, including its meaningful editable state. Freshly created
  work does not prove preservation. For first-time persistence, prove new-work
  recovery without inventing a pre-update baseline. Follow Deploy's saved-work
  contract when the release is part of the task.
- Add focused regressions for demonstrated defects. Reopen an earlier completion
  claim when new evidence contradicts it; do not preserve a checkmark over reality.

Use safe, authorized test identities and inputs. Keep synthetic activity separate
from customer/revenue metrics. Do not send customer messages, book real appointments,
create provider objects just to inspect their format, or spend money as a QA shortcut.
Payments owns the distinction between validate-only, isolated payment/delivery tests
and actual live purchases. A private test session is not evidence that login works.

## Finish the owned work

Check TODOs as their results are proved and attach concise evidence. Preserve remaining
gaps and the next action in the same plan; do not replace unfinished work with a summary.
Complete the owned behavior and relevant recovery, not just the listed edits.

Use Git and Deploy for an authorized publication. Deploy the intended committed
revision and verify what actually serves, including integrations whose callbacks or
environment require it. A local-only or validation-only task retains its diff and
evidence for the integrator; it does not silently commit, push or deploy. Do not create
empty commits or deploy after every formal planning step.

Report implemented and verified scope separately from untested behavior and customer
impact. Use EGBE's handoff for incomplete work. Where a human will verify later, use
[private review](workflows/private-review-handoff.md) so the access and steps remain
usable after the implementation agent stops.
