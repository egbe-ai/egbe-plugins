# Shape the offer

Use this when deciding what customers buy or changing a promise that creates
delivery or operating obligations. Reuse the accepted [value](value.md), customer
evidence and existing terms; an ordinary implementation or balance check does
not require a pricing exercise.

## Connect payment to experienced value

Explain why someone would choose this over their current alternative, what could
justify the first payment, and whether the job naturally recurs, accumulates or
ends with one result. A quota, renewal or repeated identical action is not repeat
value. A free result may already solve the job; more signup steps do not make a
paid offer valuable. Separate the value recipient from the payer where necessary.

Choose the model, customer-value unit, price and number of offers from that logic,
evidence, operating costs and available implementation. There is no default
subscription or tier count. Use `egbe-os-saas-unit-economics` for calculations and
`egbe-os-payments` to check the selected model's implementation, not to infer demand
from provider capabilities. Mark an untested price as a hypothesis with a useful
way to assess it, not as a proven market fact.

Every paid right must correspond to real product behavior delivered by the release.
Packaging cannot invent collaboration, ownership or features absent from its scope.
If the natural offer and available infrastructure differ, explain the mismatch,
credible alternatives and the condition for revisiting the choice; do not conceal
it behind a superficially compliant tariff.

## Make the customer's commitment clear

Set what payment enables and when it happens relative to the result. Describe
applicable delivery, continuation, limits, renewal, failure, cancellation, refund
and change-of-plan behavior. Only the transitions relevant to this offer need
decisions; uncertainty is not permission to copy a payment provider's defaults.
Keep acquired work and existing customer promises intact. A recorded payment and
the customer's useful result being delivered are separate facts.

Pass identity and ownership needs back to value; [experience](experience.md)
places the agreed steps and explains them. Keep specific terms in the accepted
requirements/offer, not as rules of this playbook.

## Check the whole obligation

Consider the founder's money, time, tools and access, including whether more funding
is permitted. Use `egbe-os-wallet` for actual balance, rates and operating policy.
Cover delivery to the last customer, support, recovery/export and applicable
refund obligations. Shortening acquisition does not automatically shorten this tail.

Distinguish fixed charges on their actual clock, estimated variable work, cash
needed before delivery, fees deducted from sales, payout timing and reversal
exposure. Unknown cost is neither zero nor a verified shortfall. Use current terms,
proportionate ranges or a small authorized measurement that can change the decision.
Label a cap as enforced, manual or only forecast; manual controls must fit the
founder's availability. Do not rely on hypothetical sales to fund current promises.

If the commitment does not fit, compare smaller breadth, lower intake, a different
prospective term or another permitted delivery method while preserving useful
value and existing rights. Name the limiting promise/resource and the next decision
or check when no credible option is established. Financial feasibility, permission
to launch and implementation completion remain separate; keep independent authorized
work moving. A free pilot may teach something without satisfying a paying-customer goal.

Keep the accepted choice, basis, material assumptions and unresolved dependencies
in the existing requirements or plan. Revisit them when scope/resources change
and before making the commitment, not on every technical operation.
