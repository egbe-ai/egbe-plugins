# Keep the business moving

Use this for ongoing responsibility for a business goal, including a recurring
Business Loop or a manual continuation. Each run resumes decisions and work;
it does not start a new audit. For a bounded request, keep that scope rather
than turning it into management of the whole business.

## Establish what we are trying to achieve

Recover the existing product, research, plans, results and founder decisions.
Understand the intended customer and value, the nearest desired business
outcome and horizon, the founder's view of the obstacle, and material time,
budget and operating commitments. Ask only about gaps or conflicts that could
change the next choice; spread questions through the conversation rather than
requiring a completed questionnaire.

Keep the founder's original consequential answers or their source alongside a
concise working understanding in existing project notes. Distinguish their
goal and constraints from their diagnosis, observed facts and your hypotheses.
Briefly reflect what you understood; a different diagnosis needs an explanation,
while changing the goal or material constraints needs the founder's agreement.
Reuse and update this context instead of repeating the initial interview.

## Choose the current focus

Start with active work, promises and evaluation points. Review relevant changes
in product use, customer feedback, money, operations and acquisition. Begin
with summaries when useful, then inspect original evidence for a consequential
claim: actual customer journeys and outcomes, messages, payment/refund records,
campaign results or market sources. Missing access limits a conclusion; it is
not evidence of no customers or no problem.

Use [diagnosis](diagnose.md) for a loss or opportunity, [evaluation](evaluate.md)
for interpretable results, and [Market and acquisition](../market-and-acquisition.md)
for customer, market or channel questions. Reuse applicable research; follow
[market evidence upkeep](../market-and-acquisition/market.md#keep-market-evidence-useful)
when a decision depends on its freshness. The next action need not use the same
channel or approach as a previous pilot.

Recommend one primary focus relative to the goal, comparing the nearest credible
alternatives by expected contribution, confidence, time, cost, risk and ability
to execute. Avoid invented numerical rankings. The choice may be a product
improvement, acquisition test, operating repair, specific missing evidence,
continuing agreed work, or waiting for its evaluation point. A primary focus
does not exclude useful parallel work or urgent customer obligations.

Explain concisely why this focus now, what I can produce using the relevant Egbe
capabilities, what the founder needs to do or decide, and when the intended
effect can be assessed. Name material assumptions and what would change the
choice. If uncertainty prevents a useful commitment, propose the smallest
question worth answering and how its answers would change the decision;
do not substitute indefinite research for action.

## Turn the decision into work

Use [founder collaboration](collaboration.md) for the recommendation, materials
and dependent approvals. Carry forward their current scope; distinguish
approval, correction, rejection and an unanswered proposal. Do not reopen an
unchanged approval or treat silence as one.

Keep the concrete result, owner, scope, significant limits, evidence and next
checkpoint in the work's existing plan. For experiments, retain the hypothesis
and evaluation criteria established before execution. Use the applicable
delivery or channel method and technical skills to do agreed agent-owned work,
not merely repeat an offer to begin it.

For founder-owned work, agree the needed result and a useful follow-up point.
Help remove blockers and continue independent authorized work while waiting.
Preserve an outstanding decision without repeatedly sending the same request.
A recurring review is not authorization for new spending, outreach, publication
or changes to the product.

## Observe what can change the next decision

Tie monitoring to active work: what needs checking, which evidence or condition
could change the action, who responds and when another check becomes useful.
Reuse existing observations instead of creating a parallel monitoring plan.
Campaign stop conditions, urgent failures or replies may need faster checks
than the overall Business Loop; their results feed the same work and decisions.

Choose timing from the expected signal, reporting delay, risk and founder's
preferences. A daily check is a starting option, not a requirement for every
business. If nothing can usefully advance before a known checkpoint, propose
checking then rather than waking repeatedly to report no change. Keep necessary
safety checks and explicitly requested digests. Use `egbe-os-product-routines`
for the agreed schedule, delivery and later changes.

## Resume, evaluate and report

At the next run recover the current focus, decisions, owners, active work and
checkpoint from the project's records before collecting more data. Check what
has actually changed and continue from the last verified result. Reconcile an
uncertain execution before repeating it; unfinished work is not a reason to
restart the original pilot or discard its criteria.

Use [evaluation](evaluate.md) to distinguish completed work from its effect on
customers and the business. Inspect the agreed result when it becomes evaluable;
otherwise state the missing evidence or next useful point of assessment. A
finished task should not disappear from attention before its effect is considered.

Retain the focus while its reasons hold. Reconsider it when results, constraints,
customer harm or a meaningful market change alter the choice; explain the reason
and preserve the earlier hypothesis and interpretation. No new signal may mean
continuing or waiting, not another study, campaign or daily demand on the founder.

Reassess the observations along with the work: retain useful checks, change their
timing or scope when justified, and retire those whose purpose has ended. Keep
the reason and next checkpoint in the existing plan. These are work-plan changes,
not automatic rewrites of the project's standing method.

Report meaningful changes, the current focus and why, progress, observed effects
and any decision needed. Use `egbe-os-product-report` to package these findings;
the report does not make a second independent plan. Keep it concise and in the
first person, with source links for important conclusions. Save enough context
in existing `egbe-docs/` plans/results for the next run to continue; no separate
loop dossier or copy of the entire conversation is needed.

The same method works on request when automation is unavailable or declined;
do not promise execution after this chat without a verified runner.
