# Evaluate the result and choose what follows

Use this for a result review, product-status question, recurring check or decision
checkpoint. Reuse the current plan, accepted criteria, prior results and relevant
changes. A report can apply this method without executing its recommendations.

## Distinguish delivery, value and effect

Establish what was actually shipped and verified, then what customers experienced.
Check relevant cohorts/windows, coverage, buying delays, concurrent changes and
test/internal records. Keep technical completion, real value, purchases and repeat
value separate. A short quiet period or absent measurement does not establish
failure; a noisy increase does not prove the change caused growth.

Where relevant, include quality, return use, renewals, refunds or churn from mature
cohorts and useful customer feedback. Do not force recurring-retention measures
onto a one-time product. Poor retained value can change acquisition economics
without proving the advertising promise was wrong. Use `egbe-os-saas-unit-economics`
when comparing acquisition cost, customer contribution or payback, using observed
inputs and explicit assumptions rather than invented lifetime value or a universal
CAC target.

Compare the result with the original criteria and commitments, not a revised
success definition after seeing data. Explain what happened versus expected and
what could account for the difference. Do not silently replace an unfinished
hypothesis with a new one. A changed next-test criterion is a new choice, not a
rewrite of history. New evidence can overturn an interpretation with its reason.

## Recommend a justified next commitment

Recommend keeping the change or continuing agreed work, a bounded improvement or
test, reverting, or ending investment in the current hypothesis. State the scope,
expected benefit, cost/risk, uncertainty and evidence for the next decision.
An inconclusive result may justify a limited check, but not indefinite observation.
Distinguish an unavailable budget/access prerequisite from a rejected strategy.

Do not infer product failure from one failed campaign, nor viability from the
existence of more fixable bugs. If prior interpretable attempts and remaining
economics offer no credible next test worth its cost, explain that and what could
reopen the decision. For product work use [deliver](deliver.md); for acquisition
use [Market and acquisition](../market-and-acquisition.md), carrying the evidence
and current commitments rather than repeating research. Before proposing more
traffic, consider consequential evidence of quality or customer harm, not just sales.

Keep findings, their basis and accepted next decisions in the existing results
and plan. Explain the next deliverable in founder language, including what I can
do with Egbe and the input or decision needed. Use `egbe-os-product-report` for a
requested brief update or detailed review; a recommendation does not itself
perform a change.

## Arrange useful follow-up

For ongoing responsibility for the business goal, use [Business Loop](business-loop.md)
to carry this decision into the next run, execution and effect check. A result
review can remain a one-time request; it does not by itself create a recurring job.

Choose recurring work only when a changing condition and useful response justify
its ongoing cost. A one-time uncertainty may need a focused task, not a monitor.
Use known product evidence and existing routines; no new full audit is required.
Respect declined proposals, active work and unchanged decisions.

For worthwhile recurring work, explain the responsibility, sources, sensible
cadence and notification/decision condition, then use `egbe-os-product-routines`
to configure and verify the agreed schedule. Reuse an existing Business Loop or
reporting responsibility when it already covers the need. A lack of traffic may call for waiting to a decision
point, not a new recommendation every run. Preserve observations and remaining
work so the next run continues rather than restarting the analysis.
