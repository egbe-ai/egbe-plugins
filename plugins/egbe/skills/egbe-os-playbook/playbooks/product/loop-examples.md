# Choose a useful cycle

Use these examples when a founder has a goal but no clear repeatable process.
They connect work to feedback, not prescribe a new business stage or a schedule.
Compare the relevant options by contribution to the goal, available evidence,
time to useful feedback and ability to act. Keep the selected approach in the
work's existing plan; no separate loop specification is needed.

## Repair a problem customers encounter

An error, failed journey or customer report points to a concrete loss. Inspect
the original evidence and affected behavior, then use `egbe-os-errors`,
`egbe-os-product-build`, `egbe-os-git` and `egbe-os-deploy` for the agreed repair
and verification. Existing work on the same problem is a continuation, not a
second task.

A tested fix and a working release establish repair evidence. Later occurrences
under comparable use determine whether the problem recurs; silence without
traffic or working error collection does not prove resolution. A recurrence
reopens diagnosis from the new evidence rather than repeating the old fix.

## Help users reach the product's value

Use `egbe-os-analytics`, relevant DB records and customer feedback to locate an
important obstacle in a real journey. Apply [diagnosis](diagnose.md) before
choosing an experience or product change; a drop-off alone does not establish
its cause. Use the relevant design/build capabilities to deliver the chosen
change, then [evaluate](evaluate.md) its effect on the intended customer outcome.

Verify that the behavior works separately from whether it helps customers.
Use the result to retain, revise or abandon the hypothesis; repeated cosmetic
changes without new evidence are not an improvement cycle.

## Learn which customers and offer fit

Use the [Market and acquisition method](../market-and-acquisition.md) to choose
the audience, evidence and test. `egbe-os-apollo-lead-finder`,
`egbe-os-instantly-campaign-launcher` and `egbe-os-mail` can support a B2B cycle
from relevant prospects to outreach and actual replies. Research and other
acquisition capabilities may fit a different business better.

Responses, objections and downstream customer outcomes inform the next test of
audience or offer. Sending successfully is operational evidence, not demand.
Distinguish delivery problems from a weak proposition before changing the
message or increasing volume; continue the concrete test's agreed conditions.

## Obtain the first signal for a new idea

With no customers or product data, identify the uncertainty that could change
what is worth building or testing. Use available founder context, market
sources and `egbe-os-search`, `egbe-os-research` or `egbe-os-scrape` to prepare a
bounded check. Depending on the question, the next evidence may come from
customer conversations, a prototype, a public offer or a small acquisition test.
Use the relevant product or acquisition method to prepare it.

The first useful result is evidence for a decision, not a complete autonomous
business. A research summary alone does not validate demand. Revisit the choice
when the planned evidence arrives; do not schedule recurring reports on metrics
that the project cannot yet produce.

## Learn without rewriting the method on every run

A result normally changes the concrete work plan. When evidence supports an
ongoing change in how the project works, propose that method adaptation with its
reason and use EGBE's existing playbook-change instructions. Rewriting a prompt
does not demonstrate improvement. Preserve uncertainty and competing explanations
when feedback is sparse or several things changed together.
