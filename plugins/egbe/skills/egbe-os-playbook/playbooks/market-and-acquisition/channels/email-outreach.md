# Email outreach

Use this for a targeted email acquisition test, whether recipients already exist
or need to be sourced. It supplements the shared market and acquisition method:
keep concrete choices and results in the existing test plan, not in this playbook.

## Recipient and source coverage

Identify whose role, situation and ability to act make the message relevant to
the test. Reuse research and an existing suitable list. With
`egbe-os-apollo-lead-finder`, choose Apollo, Places or another available source
for its actual coverage; a local business audience may be poorly represented in
a B2B people database. Inspect a small sample for relevance before paying to
enrich a larger selection.

Distinguish a suitable organisation, a likely contact and a usable email address.
Missing or partial source results do not show that the market is absent. Explain
coverage gaps and propose another source or a reasoned audience change; do not
silently broaden the audience to fill a quota. Names and personalisation must
come from evidence, not guesses from an email address or invented familiarity.

## Message and sequence

Make the first contact easy to understand: why this recipient, the useful offer
and a feasible next step. Prefer concise copy, a modest initial request and simple
formatting without unnecessary links. These are starting choices, not bans:
include a relevant resource or meeting invitation when it serves the recipient
and the test. Personal relevance does not require a name token or a fixed script.

Give each follow-up a purpose: useful context, clarification, a reminder or closing
the conversation. Choose a finite sequence and spacing for the buying context;
not every follow-up needs new material. Make declining straightforward. Use the
actual product's capabilities and proof, and do not ask for credentials or
sensitive customer files in a cold reply; use an appropriate protected route if
the product needs them.

Prepare the actual recipients, messages and intended responses for the agreed
material review. `egbe-os-instantly-campaign-launcher` translates that plan into
campaigns and replies; API acceptance does not establish copy quality.

## Readiness, pilot and pace

Usually start with part of a finite audience so an untested approach does not
consume the whole list. Size the pilot and subsequent batches using available
recipients, contact costs, sending capacity and the ability to handle replies.
Expand only as delivery and responses justify it, rather than meeting a universal
contact target or daily minimum. A sourced email is not yet a campaign-ready
recipient: check suitability, prior contact, refusals and address verification.

Use the Instantly skill for technical readiness and delivery protection. In the
plan, explain the chosen volume, schedule, operational stop conditions and who
will handle replies. Distinguish new recipients from all sent messages, including
follow-ups. State whether a limit applies to one campaign, each sender or the
project as a whole; account for other campaigns sharing those senders or limits.
A per-campaign cap is not a project-wide safeguard. Check delivery problems early;
judge the offer later when the relevant recipients have had time to receive and
respond. One fixed review
hour or contact count cannot serve both purposes. Arrange real observation using
the shared [test workflow](../run-test.md), without assuming every optional job
or daily document is needed.

## Continue the conversation

Read the actual reply and thread. Distinguish interest, a question, an objection,
a refusal, an automatic response and a different contact. Continue from what the
person said and what the product can deliver, not from a keyword category.
Keep promises consistent with the offer and carry relevant context into a handoff.
When a founder decision is needed, provide the reply, a proposed response and why.

Use the Instantly reply tools to preserve the thread and sender, record confirmed
refusals and check that sending has stopped. An automatic reply is not a person
declining, and marking a queue item handled is not proof of a provider-side stop.

## Interpret the test

Separate delivery, substantive replies, qualified interest and the intended
product action. Show the counts behind rates and distinguish people from messages;
automatic replies, refusals and follow-ups do not all represent new interest.
Use product analytics for the corresponding action when available. Opens are at
most a noisy diagnostic, not proof of attention, demand or a winning message;
disabled tracking yields no open-rate evidence.

Compare recipients with comparable time to receive the sequence and respond.
Recently added batches are not mature just because the campaign is old. Identify
whether the observed problem is source relevance, delivery, the offer/message,
conversation handling or the subsequent product path. Aggregate campaign counts
cannot attribute success to the last follow-up or rank unmeasured message variants.

Use [evaluation](../evaluate.md) for the supported next decision. Do not convert
a universal reply-rate band into an automatic scale/pivot verdict; retain useful
reply evidence and the interpretation in the existing results.
