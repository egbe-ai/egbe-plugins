# Research analysis and adaptation

Use the assignment to decide which observations matter. The following are
analytical lenses, not a required checklist for every video.

## Compare evidence in context

Compare relevant examples, including contrary cases. Account size, audience,
publication time, distribution, sponsorship and missing metrics can explain
differences; descriptive ratios do not isolate a creative's causal effect.
Separate observed facts, plausible interpretations and unanswered questions.
Do not turn a selected sample into a claim about the whole platform.

Start each finding from exact source evidence, then group observations that
actually share the claimed mechanism. Explain what they have in common and the
limits of that comparison. Repetition alone does not establish generality or
success. No minimum number of sources turns an interpretation into a fact.

## Inspect the parts relevant to the question

- **Opening and promise:** distinguish what can catch attention in the first
  frame from the reason to keep watching. Check how the later material delivers
  that promise. Support visual claims with inspected frames and spoken claims
  with transcript evidence, not captions or engagement totals.
- **Demonstration and proof:** identify what is shown, what is merely asserted,
  and how a source establishes credibility. Attribute its datasets, rankings,
  testimonials and results to that source; they are not the product's proof.
- **Format, message and action:** describe the observable structure and intended
  viewer action. Consider alternatives such as a demonstration, comparison,
  explanation or recurring format only when they fit the evidence and product.
- **Sound and context:** consider how audio relates to the scene, audience and
  meaning when evidence allows. A sound's age or one video's views do not prove
  it is rising, saturated or responsible for performance.
- **Audience language:** distinguish comments about the intended buyer/problem
  from jokes, creator affinity and unrelated topics. Quote or reference actual
  comments; topic analogy is not buyer relevance. Visible commenters are a
  selected sample, not a demand survey.

## Propose grounded adaptations

Explain which source mechanism is useful for this product and why. Keep the
observed example separate from a drafted opening, promise or demonstration.
Adapt the mechanism rather than copying a creator's proprietary evidence or
promising their performance.

Consider the product's real assets, production effort, repeatability and audience
fit. When an idea depends on a customer result, dataset, footage or capability
that is not available, state that dependency or propose an honest alternative.
A series, character, comparison or list is an option—not a required format.

Return only adaptations justified by the assignment and sources. Avoid numerical
impact/difficulty scores, fixed idea quotas and universal creative rules without
evidence. The result informs the product's next decision; it does not itself
choose a business goal, authorize publishing or prove growth.
